## The Altar and the Machine: Technology, Meaning and the Sacred (2027)

_(forthcoming, James Clarke) &nbsp;_

A cultural critique of the modern technological crisis. Drawing on Heidegger and cognitive science, I argue that civilization has organized itself around instrumental reason, treating everything as a standing reserve to be optimized and stripping reality of its sacred character. AI is the apex of this: systems possessing immense intelligence but no understanding, no care, no grounding in the Good. When competitive pressures ensure no one slows down, and the systems guiding consequential decisions have no concept of what those decisions are for, the crisis is not technical but spiritual.

The solution is not to reject technology but to subordinate it to something higher. The book develops a philosophy of sacred making, the difference between building that serves only itself and building oriented toward what transcends it, and argues for recovering a mode of being that prioritizes meaning over efficiency.&nbsp;

It grows out of a series of essays called _The Age of Spiritual Blindness_, expanding and tightening those arguments. The original essays are available upon request.

## In Search of the Infinite: A Psychedelic Memoir (2025)

Documents a decade-long experiment with consciousness and the gradual shift from rigid scientific materialism to a religious worldview, through the lens of philosophy and aided by psychedelic experience. A real-time record of the struggle to reconcile mystical experience with a skeptical intellect, moving beyond scientific reductionism into the realm of ontology and confronting what these experiences actually mean for how we understand mind, reality, and the self.

The central argument is that science, while indispensable for explaining the mechanisms of the material world, cannot account for the full depth of human experience. For that, we require the religious practices, symbols, and ways of being refined over millennia to navigate what lies beyond the reach of measurement.
