## **Enactive Machines, Intelligence vs. Rationality, and Why Psychedelics Go Wrong** | Rahul Sam (2026)

I joined Rahul Sam for a conversation on philosophy, psychedelics, AI, and the limits of technological thinking. We began with my move from neuroscience into philosophy and the gap between scientific expertise and philosophical understanding. From there, we discussed Heidegger, the technological framing of life, and my critique of Bryan Johnson’s psychedelic experiment, especially the way psychedelic insights can be absorbed back into a culture of optimization, data, and self management. We then turned to integration, asking why psychedelic experiences often fail to change people unless they are received within a durable practice, tradition, or way of life. We discussed AI, the distinction between intelligence and rationality, why LLMs struggle with framing and relevance, and why treating machines as models of human cognition distorts our understanding of what human beings are. &nbsp;

## **Rediscovering the Sacred in a Technological World** | _Conversations for the End_ with Connor (2026)

A wide-ranging conversation that moves from psychedelic memoir to philosophy of technology. We opened with _In Search of the Infinite_: what it actually is as a book, how it was written in real time during sessions over seven years, and why I resist the reading that I turned religious simply because of mystical experiences on substances. From there we got into the phenomenology of the sacred, the ineffable quality of altered states, and what the loss of my mother forced me to confront about what is actually real. The second half turned to technology and spiritual blindness: why dystopia is not a future spectacle but a creeping normality we already inhabit, how impersonal systems like AI develop their own logic beyond any individual's control, and why the internet functions as a hall of mirrors that inverts the outward orientation that both psychedelics and religious traditions demand. We also worked through the being versus having distinction, limbic capitalism, why LLMs are technically engines of bullshit in Frankfurt's precise sense, and what reengaging with the sacred might actually require given how thoroughly technological thinking has colonized even our spiritual intuitions.

## **What Is the Use of God, Life Outside Religion, and AI** | _Where I Belong_ with Avinash Sai (2025)

A second conversation with Avinash, picking up several threads from the first. We opened with the problem of evil: if God gave humans free will and won't intervene, what is the point of God at all? I worked through why that question is harder than it looks, including what it would actually mean to design a world without suffering and why that thought experiment tends to collapse. We talked about whether religious belief gets in the way of critical thinking, and why I think the relationship between religiosity and rationality is far messier empirically than the standard secular narrative admits. I also shared something personal about my mother's illness and how I came to see faith and hope as less distinguishable than I once thought. From there we moved to AI: why I think it is genuinely useful but likely catastrophic, how it erodes the very interior of the self, and why I think a religious orientation is one of the few things that can actually function as a shield against the worst of it.

## **Religion as a Map of Reality, Cults, and Psychedelics** | _Where I Belong_ with Avinash Sai (2025)

I joined Avinash to talk about religion, cults, and psychedelics. We started with the common perception of religion as comfort and why I think that misreads what serious religious commitment actually demands. From there we got into what the New Atheists genuinely get right, and where their critique collapses when you look at the deeper tradition. We discussed why cults mimic religion's appeal but lack its moral and traditional grounding. On psychedelics, I explained the basic neuroscience, why the "more real than real" quality of the experience is philosophically significant and tends to get ignored in clinical framings, and the value of shamanic traditions for providing cosmological context that the secular West simply doesn't have. We also talked about Jordan Peterson: what his early synthesis got right and why things went sideways.
