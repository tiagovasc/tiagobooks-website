## **Enactive Machines, Intelligence vs. Rationality, and Why Psychedelics Go Wrong** | Rahul Sam (2026)

I joined Rahul Sam for a conversation on philosophy, psychedelics, AI, and the limits of technological thinking. We discussed Heidegger and my critique of Bryan Johnson’s psychedelic experiment, especially the way psychedelic insights can be absorbed back into a culture of optimization. We then turned to AI, the distinction between intelligence and rationality, and why treating machines as models of human cognition distorts our understanding of what human beings are.

## **Rediscovering the Sacred in a Technological World** | _Conversations for the End_ with Connor (2026)

A wide-ranging conversation that moves from psychedelic memoir to philosophy of technology. We opened with _In Search of the Infinite_: how it was written in real time during sessions over seven years, and why I resist the reading that I turned religious simply because of mystical experiences on substances. The second half turned to technology and spiritual blindness: why dystopia is not a future spectacle but a creeping normality we already inhabit, why the internet functions as a hall of mirrors, why LLMs are technically engines of bullshit in Frankfurt's precise sense, and what reengaging with the sacred might actually require.

## **What Is the Use of God, Life Outside Religion, and AI** | _Where I Belong_ with Avinash Sai (2025)

A second conversation with Avinash. We opened with the problem of evil: if God gave humans free will and won't intervene, what is the point of God at all? I also shared something personal about my mother's illness and how I came to see faith and hope as less distinguishable than I once thought. From there we moved to AI: why I think it is genuinely useful but likely catastrophic, and why a religious orientation is one of the few things that can function as a shield against the worst of it.

## **Religion as a Map of Reality, Cults, and Psychedelics** | _Where I Belong_ with Avinash Sai (2025)

I joined Avinash to talk about religion, cults, and psychedelics. We started with the common perception of religion as comfort and why I think that misreads what serious religious commitment actually demands, and why cults mimic religion's appeal but lack its moral and traditional grounding. On psychedelics, I explained why the "more real than real" quality of the experience is philosophically significant. We also talked about Jordan Peterson: what his early synthesis got right and why things went sideways.
