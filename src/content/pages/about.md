Hi!

I’m a PhD candidate at the Global Centre for Advanced Studies (GCAS), supervised by John Vervaeke. My research focuses on psychedelic experience and its long-term integration, drawing on philosophy of religion, Heideggerian phenomenology, neuroscience, and 4E cognitive science. My wider interests also include philosophy of technology and mystical experience. I examine how religious traditions contribute to personal transformation, arguing that orientation toward a transcendent ideal of the Good is essential to lasting flourishing.

I hold an MSc in Applied Neuroscience from King’s College London’s Institute of Psychiatry, Psychology & Neuroscience, where I researched psychedelic therapy for depression, following undergraduate studies in philosophy and psychology.

I’m the author of _In Search of the Infinite_, a memoir combining autobiography with philosophical inquiry into altered states, mysticism, and identity. It explores how these experiences can be integrated into a scientific worldview that takes religious tradition seriously. My forthcoming book, _The Altar and the Machine: Technology, Meaning and the Sacred_, examines the philosophical assumptions shaping technological modernity and their consequences for human agency and meaning. It argues that theology is uniquely equipped to challenge the reduction of human life to metrics and computation.

I also host the _Anagoge Podcast_, featuring conversations with philosophers, theologians, and scientists about consciousness, religion, and meaning. Guests have included John Vervaeke, Graham Harman, Jonathan Pageau, and John Cottingham. Previously, I served as an Honorary Research Assistant for Drug Science, writing about contemporary psychedelic research for a wider audience.
