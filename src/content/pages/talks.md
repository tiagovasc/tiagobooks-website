## **Intelligence is not Rationality: A Cognitive-Scientific Diagnosis of the Alignment Problem (2026)**

https://www.youtube.com/embed/D6hjtY0cm3s?rel=1

Given at the 6th International Conference on Philosophy of Mind in Porto, this lecture argues that contemporary AI discourse misframes alignment by confusing intelligence with rationality. It begins with the strange profile of LLMs, which can achieve extraordinary results while failing in surprisingly elementary ways. Drawing on cognitive science, decision theory, phenomenology, and enactivist philosophy, I distinguish small world computation from large world judgment, showing why systems that operate brilliantly inside supplied frames cannot generate relevance, truth orientation, or care for the real. The talk moves through the frame problem, relevance realization, the 4P model of knowing, the Chinese Room, and recent evidence on hallucination, deception, and weak world models. It concludes that alignment is not merely an engineering problem, because objective specification already presupposes the rational judgment that current AI systems lack.

## The Sacred as Participatory Disclosure: Psychedelic Experience, 4E Cognition and The Role of Attention (2026)

https://www.youtube.com/embed/kS0zMHOjHoY?rel=1

Given at the International Interdisciplinary Conference of Psychedelic Studies (IICPS 2026) organized by the University of Bucharest, this talk argues that the philosophy of psychedelics has been asking the wrong question. Rather than treating the mystical experience as a proposition to be judged true or false, whether reducible to brain dynamics or evidence for revised metaphysics, I frame it as a participatory disclosure of a meaningful world. Drawing on Heidegger, Gibson's ecological psychology, and 4E cognitive science, I argue that set and setting are causes as real as the molecule, and that the field's focus on ego dissolution misses the harder question of what the self reorganizes toward. Bringing in Simone Weil and Iris Murdoch, I argue that attention is morally structured, and that durable transformation requires not just an experience plus integration, but a whole ecology of attention: telos, ritual, exemplars, and community, of the kind religious traditions have long provided.

## Philosophy of Psychedelics: Why Religion Matters (2026)

https://www.youtube.com/embed/brb4CdKladM?rel=1

This lecture traces the history and science of psychedelics from ancient traditions through the contemporary renaissance, establishing striking clinical results across depression, PTSD, addiction, and end-of-life anxiety. People consistently rate these experiences among the most meaningful of their lives. The talk examines key philosophical problems including the "comforting delusion" objection, ego dissolution, whether altered states yield knowledge, and naturalism versus metaphysical openness. It critiques the field's dominant medical model, which treats psychedelics as tools for fixing pathology rather than catalysts for human flourishing. Instead, it proposes that genuine transformation requires combining the revelatory power of psychedelics with the ritual, community, and vision of transcendent transformation that religious traditions provide. &nbsp;

## Music and The Human Experience (2024)

https://www.youtube.com/embed/iuoWSRVbfEs?rel=1

Delivered at the Kitarika guitar festival in Slovenia, this talk explores the profound relationship between music and the nature of reality. It challenges purely materialist perspectives by suggesting that music serves as a direct engagement with the fundamental patterns of existence and meaning. I draw upon a diverse constellation of ideas ranging from classical philosophy and theology to modern neuroscience and cognitive science to argue that music acts as a vital bridge to the sacred. I examine how our minds perceive complexity through concepts like insight and flow while tracing the historical role of sound in spiritual traditions to illustrate why music holds such a transformative power in human life.&nbsp;
