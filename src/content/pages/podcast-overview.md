The Anagoge Podcast explores philosophy, theology, consciousness, and the human condition through long-form conversations with researchers, academics, and independent thinkers. The name comes from the Greek for "upward leading," the soul's ascent toward deeper understanding. If you care about what actually orients a human life, and how we come to know anything at all, this is the place.

It’s available on [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@AnagogePodcast), [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/anagoge-podcast/id1512482918?uo=4), [Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/show/4ifa95kknLrUQblYTUMQ7T), and most other podcast platforms.

https://www.youtube.com/embed/6_DM-OseSc0?rel=1

The conversations range widely but circle a few deep questions: What is consciousness, and can science account for it? What did the ancients understand about wisdom that we have forgotten? Why does the sacred keep returning, even in a secular age? Topics move from ancient philosophy, Platonism, and mysticism to cognitive science, phenomenology, psychedelics, and the meaning crisis, always with an eye toward how these ideas bear on how we actually live.

Guests have included cognitive scientist John Vervaeke on wisdom and the meaning crisis, iconographer Jonathan Pageau on religious symbolism, philosopher Graham Harman on object-oriented ontology, and neuroscientist Joseph LeDoux on the science of emotion. Other conversations feature Shaun Gallagher on phenomenology and 4E cognition, Peter Sjöstedt-H on panpsychism and psychedelics, Gregg Henriques on unifying psychology, John Cottingham on the return of the sacred, philosopher of science Michael Strevens, and Gregory Sadler on teaching philosophy.

Each episode is an unhurried, serious attempt to think alongside someone who has spent a life on these questions. No hot takes, no clickbait. Just careful, curious dialogue about the things that matter most.
