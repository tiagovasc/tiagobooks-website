**_A book review of&nbsp;‘_Zombies in Western Culture_’ by_ Christopher Mastropietro, Filip Miscevic, and John Vervaeke**

I have been a fan of Vervaeke for a while, and I was excited when he finally published a book. The work is largely within the theme of his newest and most famous lecture series regarding the meaning crisis, although here it explores it in the very particular context of the zombie myth. Myths provide structure to culture because, in narrative form, they give us an existential map of why we are here, where we came from, and what will happen in the future. While myths can have many forms, they often include monsters, and those monsters have several symbolic purposes which aren’t necessarily explicit. For example, in the 20th century, the alien was one of the most popular monster stories, representing the fear of the "other" or the foreigner. The book argues that our current myth is the zombie, and it is the monster that represents our society.

The zombie symbol is the anti-tragedy. In the classical Greek tragedy, suffering nevertheless can still have meaning and purpose, but most zombie stories do not have an end. It is a perversion of the Christian mythos of death and resurrection where there is no salvation and no redemption. Zombies have no language. They lack Logos and cannot transmit truth. They are not self-aware, meaning they can’t represent themselves and have meaningful cognition that frames the environment. They eat brains because they are hungry to have a "mind" and to acquire intelligibility, yet they can never have it. They are stuck in an eternal and hopeless cycle of wanting. No matter how much they eat, their hunger persists. Furthermore, they do not have a home and don’t belong anywhere, drifting in a completely random manner. Zombies are all isolated from each other even though they swarm. They are infected, and infection is transmitted by touch. It is the antithesis of intimacy, and it is hard if not impossible to avoid getting infected.

They are such a powerful symbol because zombies are us, the modern man. They are a monster of nihilism. All the features that the zombie has are a representation of what our culture and society have become and where it is headed. The authors explore this connection in great detail, going through each point and explaining the causal connection of both the myth to the culture and what led to our current cultural paradigm. It is very well written and well-argued, and I highly recommend it.
