title: The Ground We Cannot Grasp
date: 2026-05-23 04:18:00-05:00
tags:
- General
---

> This dialogue gives form to conversations I have been having for years around reality, knowledge, love, emptiness, and the possibility of transcendence, especially by way of phenomenology, cognitive science, Christianity, and Buddhism.
> 
> It draws most directly from recent conversations with a friend who was very influenced by Madhyamaka Buddhism and the enactive tradition in cognitive science, though neither speaker represents a fixed doctrine and both positions are partly fictionalized.
> 
> I have cast it in the form of a Platonic or Socratic dialogue because the questions themselves seemed to come alive only through tension, disagreement, and mutual correction. The aim is not to resolve these problems, but to lay out what feel to me like the central dilemmas of metaphysics, of contemplative practice, and of the human search for what is ultimately real.

**TIAGO.** I want to put something to you and have you take it apart, if you can. Two questions keep coming back to me and they will not separate. What is real, and how could we ever know it? I cannot shake a certain answer to the first. Some things seem to me simply, immutably real. I do not mean useful assumptions, or lines we draw because drawing them is convenient. I mean something more like a wall you bang your head against. We come up against them; we do not build them. They were the case before we arrived and will be the case after we leave. Tell me where that goes wrong.

**MAREK.** Let me start with your second question, because I think it unsettles the first. If you cannot build this wall, and you cannot get behind it to check, how would you ever know it is there?

**TIAGO.** Barely, is the honest answer, and that does not trouble me. My tradition has said for a very long time that our knowledge of the ultimate is partial, mediated, always failing. The whole discipline of negation, saying only what God is not because we cannot say what God is, admits the gap between what is and what we can put into words. But you have asked two things at once. Whether I can see the wall clearly, and whether the wall is there at all. I give up the first cheerfully. It is the second I will not give up.

**MAREK.** Before we get to whether it is there, let me complicate that second question of yours further. The knowing you are worried about, the kind that goes into words, was never where the depth was anyway.

**TIAGO.** Say more.

**MAREK.** There is the knowing that lives in a skill, which you cannot write down and hand to someone. There is the knowing of a perspective, of what the world is like from inside a particular situation. And there is the knowing that comes only by taking part in something, where you and the thing change each other as you go. We are inside one of those right now. None of it is the kind that turns into propositions, and all of it runs deeper than the propositions we will reach for later. So when you say we can barely know the ultimate, I would answer: in propositions, almost not at all. But that was always the shallow end of knowing.

**TIAGO.** On that we do not disagree in the slightest. But notice what follows. If knowing is not only a matter of propositions, then reality does not reach us only by appearing as an object we can define. It may also reach us by what refuses to move when we test it, and by what we find ourselves unable to betray without ceasing to tell the truth about our own life. The real is not only what stands there to be inspected. It is also what discloses the world more deeply when we participate in it. If a way of living makes more reality visible, and if betraying it makes us less truthful, then that too may be a way reality gives itself to be known.

**MAREK.** That already sounds like you are moving from humility about knowledge to confidence about being.

**TIAGO.** Perhaps. But the movement is not careless. There are two ways reality presses on us. One is by remaining what it is no matter what we wish. The other is by becoming the thing before which our wishes are judged. A stone resists my hand from outside. A promise resists me from within. Both are forms of resistance, and I am not sure the second is less revealing than the first.

**MAREK.** I can grant the phenomenology. I do not yet grant the metaphysics.

**TIAGO.** Nor am I asking you to grant it too quickly. Then let me make the question as ordinary as I can. The test is not whether something overwhelms us in a single moment, but whether it survives the return to the ordinary morning. We are not neutral observers of a finished world. We live toward something. Even before theology enters, human life has the shape of answerability. We are pulled by what we are becoming and judged by possibilities we have not yet reached. Some failures shame us; other goods summon us in ways we did not choose. Orientation is not optional for a sane life. The question is whether that orientation answers only to projects we invent, or whether some projects answer to something real enough to judge them.

**MAREK.** I will defend that structure even where I leave your conclusion. We are creatures shaped by what is ahead of us. The self you are trying to become is already leaning on the self you are now, asking things of it. Orientation is not optional. The feeling of facing something you did not choose, I believe that too. It is the fixed pole I will not simply hand over.

**TIAGO.** Then let me give you the one case where I cannot pry the feeling apart from the fact. Love.

**MAREK.** What kind of love? Affection, attachment, desire, loyalty, romance? Because if you begin there, I can already see several ways the argument could go wrong.

**TIAGO.** Not only those, though it may pass through all of them. I mean love in its deepest, agapic sense, love as self giving rather than possession, the love in which the other is not merely wanted, enjoyed, possessed, or useful, but disclosed as irreducibly real. The love before which the self becomes answerable. The love in which the Good becomes personal, and does not simply add one more preference to the world, but changes the field in which preferences are judged. I cannot make myself treat the love I have for my fiancée as a useful construct that, under some other arrangement of things, might have turned out not to matter. The depth of it is bound up with the fact that it cannot be taken apart. If it could be, it would not be what it is.

**MAREK.** That might be only psychology talking. Attachment, habit, the stories we tell ourselves to stay steady.

**TIAGO.** It might, but I do not think so, and here is the difference. A commitment you can dissolve on reflection is a different kind of thing from one you cannot. The sense that it cannot be undone is already inside it, part of what makes it what it is. So when I ask whether love is contingent in some final sense, the answer my life gives back is that it cannot be, and I take that as telling me something about what love is, not just about how I happen to feel. Not because inward certainty is enough by itself. Lives can lie to themselves. But because this answer does not merely intensify my feeling. It makes me see more, evade less, possess less, and become more answerable to what is not me.

**MAREK.** But many things have felt indissoluble to people. A nation, a wound, an ideology, an obsession. They too can speak with the voice of necessity.

**TIAGO.** Yes. And that is why feeling necessary is not enough. Some commitments tighten the world around us, leaving us smaller and more afraid. Others widen it. They make us answerable rather than merely possessed, and hand back more reality than we had before. If love is the clearest form in which the Good becomes personal, then even its distortions tell us something. The false necessities you name are not arguments against the Good. They are parasitic on it. A nation does not bind people by saying it is arbitrary. An ideology does not usually announce itself as delusion. A wound does not command us merely as pain, but as a claim for justice, recognition, or repair. Even an obsession borrows the structure of ultimacy from something higher than itself. These things deceive precisely by imitating righteousness, loyalty, truth, or salvation. Their force is not proof that the Good is unreal. It is proof that the Good can be misapprehended, displaced, and made idolatrous. Love is strange because it does not merely bind me to a preference. It clarifies the field in which preferences are judged.

**MAREK.** Or it clarifies because you have committed. A life gathers around what it gives itself to. The sharpening tells you that you committed, not that the thing was a wall.

**TIAGO.** But then what judges the commitment? Anyone can commit. What makes one vow fidelity and another delusion, one surrender holy and another mere submission? If there is no real measure beyond the act of committing, then commitment has intensity, but not truth.

**MAREK.** I would not say there is no measure. I would say the measure is not a fixed object standing outside the relation. A commitment is tested by what it does to the one who bears it and to the world it opens. Does it loosen possession or deepen it? Does it make compassion possible or impossible? Does it survive mutual correction? Does it reduce self deception, or sanctify it? Does it return you to the world with more freedom, more tenderness, and more truth? That is how I would test a vow against a delusion.

**TIAGO.** And I would ask why those tests have authority. Why compassion rather than domination? Why self deception as a failure rather than simply one more mode of being? Why tenderness as a sign of truth rather than weakness? And why freedom, if freedom is not answerable to anything beyond its own movement? You have not escaped the question of measure. You have only moved it inside the relation.

**MAREK.** Let me set my own picture down beside that, then, because so far you have only had mine in silhouette. The word that shaped me is empty, but empty does not mean unreal. It means empty of intrinsic existence. No thing stands on its own as a finished object. Nothing has its being sealed inside itself, apart from conditions and relations, from bodies and needs, from the histories and practices that shape it. Reality is not a warehouse of self standing objects waiting to be named. It is the arising of things in relation.

**TIAGO.** And this is where your Buddhism and your cognitive science meet.

**MAREK.** Where they meet for me, yes. The Buddhist language says that things arise dependently. The enactive language says that a world shows up for a living being through its activity, its needs, its capacities, its way of moving. A cell already cuts its surroundings into food and not food. An animal finds paths, dangers, affordances. We inherit much older ways of world making before we ever speak about truth. There is no object out there independent of all participation, and no subject in here independent of the world.

**TIAGO.** But not that the world is unreal.

**MAREK.** Never that. That is the distinction I will keep making until you stop hearing empty as thinner than real. The world is fully real. It is simply real in relation rather than on its own, real as process rather than as a thing. A thing can be wholly present, wholly able to act on you, wholly worthy of devotion, and still be empty in exactly that sense.

**TIAGO.** And why does it matter so much to you that I see it that way rather than mine?

**MAREK.** Because the instant you freeze one of these relations into a wall, into something that would still be standing there with no one to meet it, you have made the move my tradition most fears. The grasping mind takes its own construction and turns it into something it must defend. The defending is where the cruelty begins. The quiet step from “this binds me” to “therefore there is an ultimate ground at the center of things” is exactly that move, made without noticing.

**TIAGO.** Then what am I supposed to stand on, once you have pulled the wall out from under me?

**MAREK.** Nothing you can stand on in that sense. You orient yourself toward love, toward the good, toward the sacred, and you take it with the whole of your seriousness, but you hold it knowing that what you are facing is a relation you are inside of, not a pillar set up outside you.

**TIAGO.** That sounds dangerously close to acting as though something is true while believing it is not.

**MAREK.** It is the opposite of that. Act as though it matters, because it does. Commit with your full weight. And still refuse to mistake the orientation for an absolute sitting apart from you. The “as if” is not pretending. It is seeing that believing and taking part are not, in the end, two separate things.

**TIAGO.** And somehow you keep your freedom inside that.

**MAREK.** That is the part I find most beautiful, and there is real cognitive science underneath it. To be alive at all is to keep drawing lines: me and not me, mine and not mine. You cannot stop while you are living. But a creature complex enough can learn to watch itself in the act of drawing the line, to hold the line and loosen its grip on it at the same time. The work is not to stop drawing lines, which would be to stop living. It is to draw one, commit to it, carry its weight, and keep the freedom to redraw it if it stops serving the truth. So there is no final ground under any of it. There is only the unfolding, and your freedom in how you move through it.

**TIAGO.** Then let me bring back the one thing I told you I could not take apart, and hold it against exactly that. If love, for creatures like us, could not have been otherwise, how is it not the wall I started with, sitting right there in the middle of your unfolding?

**MAREK.** Here is what I will give you without a struggle. There is no objective standpoint, no view from nowhere. But that does not mean objectivity is mere agreement. For creatures like us, objectivity is what becomes stable across embodied perspectives, shared vulnerability, practical resistance, and mutual correction. We share bodies, needs, mortality, the predicament of having to live alongside one another. Love is one of the universals that falls out of that. So yes, love holds. As long as there are beings like us, that line holds, and I have no wish to erase it.

**TIAGO.** But that is exactly where I hesitate. If love holds only because we are the kind of creatures we happen to be, then arrange the creature differently and the universal loosens. That is a very stable form of life. It is not yet a wall.

**MAREK.** Then perhaps I have been speaking too narrowly. The love you mean is not only affection, desire, or attachment, though it passes through all of these. I mean the love before which the self becomes answerable, the love that breaks the fantasy of being self enclosed and opens the person onto what exceeds possession.

**TIAGO.** Go carefully, because that sounds like a great deal more than groundlessness usually allows.

**MAREK.** It is more than a thin groundlessness, yes. But perhaps that is only because you keep hearing groundlessness as deprivation. Pressed far enough, the distinctions we cling to begin to thin. Self and other, possession and loss, even love and its apparent opposites become less stable than they seem from the ordinary view. That is a bitter pill, and I have not fully swallowed it myself. But love, in the sense I am reaching for, is not one thing among things. It is closer to the movement by which beings open beyond themselves. Nor is it the opposite of hate in the ordinary psychological sense. It is the loosening of the enclosed self, the thing that makes compassion possible at all. That gives love a special status, I grant you that. I do not think it is just one preference among others, or one useful fiction among others. But I still do not think its special status requires that it stand by itself.

**TIAGO.** That is far more than I expected you to grant.

**MAREK.** It is. But it is still not a pole. This larger love does not stand off by itself, an object added to the world. It is the opening of relation itself. You cannot pull it free from the weave and hold it up as the reason the weave exists.

**TIAGO.** Then I cannot see what is left of your groundlessness except a preference. You have just handed me a love woven into the structure of things.

**MAREK.** Woven in as the weaving, not as a thread you could pull free and hold up to the light. From where I stand the groundlessness is untouched.

**TIAGO.** Here is where I actually catch, then. Grant me your own claim, that there is nothing outside the relation of mind and world. I mostly do grant it. Then I cannot point to a beyond against which my line would be only provisional. The beyond is not a distant place. It is the absence of any reality in which the question could still be asked. So what is the warning in the word groundless still doing? If the relation is the end, if there is nowhere further to stand and call it provisional, then the relation is not a mere appearance of the ground. It is where things bottom out. You want to say that because the ground is not outside the meeting, it is not really ground. I want to say the opposite. If the meeting is the only world in which anything can appear, then what is ultimate must appear there, or nowhere. The fact that it is disclosed in relation does not make it less ultimate. It only means that relation is the place where the ultimate is given.

**MAREK.** Go on.

**TIAGO.** Calling it groundless because nothing lies past it starts to feel like guarding against a skeptic who never came into the room. The skeptic worth fearing is the one who says the line is arbitrary, that you could have drawn it anywhere, that love could have been otherwise. And you have just told me that love could not have been otherwise for beings like us. Once you say that, I do not know what empty is still doing, beyond warning us not to clutch at what we love.

**MAREK.** That warning is not small.

**TIAGO.** I know. It may be indispensable. But I am asking whether the discipline of not clutching has become a denial of the very thing it means to protect. Those are not the same act. I know you mean groundlessness ontologically as well as spiritually. I am not pretending it is only a therapeutic caution. But I suspect the discipline has begun to govern the ontology too completely. Because we cannot possess the ground, you say there is no ground. Because the hand closes on nothing, you say there was nothing there. I accept the warning against the hand. I do not accept the denial of what the hand was reaching toward.

**MAREK.** Be careful what you are asking, because the question is hiding a picture. I do not mean a thing in itself sitting behind a veil, waiting forever beyond reach. Nor do I mean a hidden substance that transcends phenomena from elsewhere and secretly props them up. I mean the emptiness of phenomena themselves, the fact that things arise only through conditions and cannot be grasped as self standing. The hand closes on nothing.

**TIAGO.** But that is an answer about knowing, not about being. I am not asking whether I can grasp it. I am asking whether it is there when no one is grasping at all.

**MAREK.** Then, plainly, no. Not as a thing standing on its own with no one to meet it. It is there because there is participation. Do not hear that as mind inventing the world. That would be too simple. I mean that world and mind arise together in the meeting. Pull every mind out and you do not find a bare ground left lying there. You get no meeting, no appearing, no world in the sense we are speaking of world. That is what the word groundless is doing. It is not a theory only. It is a discipline. The instant you treat the orientation as a fixed object you can hold in your hand, you have already lost it.

**TIAGO.** Then perhaps this is where we differ. You ask what love is once every condition has been stripped away. I ask what love is inside the only world where anything can appear at all. You remove the meeting and ask what remains. I ask what remains binding within the meeting.

**MAREK.** That sounds like a way to protect your wall from the one test that would show whether it is ultimate.

**TIAGO.** Or your test destroys the field in which ultimacy could ever mean anything. Pull every mind out, as you say, and you do not uncover a hidden ground. You reach the absence of any standpoint at all, which is not a vantage and not a place. But that is exactly my point. The joined world of mind and what it meets is not one reality with a truer nothing underneath it. It is the whole of reality as it can be given. It is the floor, where things bottom out, with no further down to go. The wall I began with was never beneath that floor or behind it. It stands inside it. Not inside it as a local arrangement we happened to draw, but inside it as what cannot be made arbitrary once it has been disclosed.

**MAREK.** And love is that wall.

**TIAGO.** If anything within the only world there is cannot be otherwise, cannot be dissolved while a personal standpoint remains capable of truth, then yes. It is not provisional in the ordinary sense, and it is not merely a construct. It is among the most real things there are. It stood before I came and will stand after I go, not behind the world but in the middle of it. That does not mean it floats outside relation like a second world. It means relation is not a solvent. It does not make whatever appears within it less real. If love is disclosed there as what judges me, then the disclosure is not disqualified by taking place in the only place disclosure can occur. Your groundless only denies a ground beneath the world in that sense. I never wanted that. I want to know whether there is something in the world that judges us, and I say love is that thing.

**MAREK.** Then we have walked the argument down to its last room, and I will meet you in it rather than slip out. I will not pretend there is nothing within the world that holds. There are things that do not pass while recognizably human standpoints remain, and love is among the steadiest of them. But I hold even those as standing patterns of the weaving, real exactly as the weave is real, not as pillars driven into a floor. Real, and empty, in the one breath. So we have narrowed it to almost nothing, and the almost nothing will not close. What you call a wall, I call the steadiest knot in a net that hangs from no peg.

**TIAGO.** And yet, for all that distance, here is where we strangely meet. On the discipline itself, on the refusal to grasp, we are barely apart.

**MAREK.** The finger that points at the moon is not the moon. Every tradition that lasts has its own form of that warning. My negations, your theology of saying only what God is not, the koan, the Sufi poem. All of them train the practitioner not to mistake the concept of the thing for the thing.

**TIAGO.** Completely, on the discipline. What I never grant is that the discipline has to run all the way down into denying that there is anything real there at all. There are two different warnings hiding in the same words. One says the ground is real and our grip on it is poor, so stay humble about the grip. That one I take whole. The other says the very idea of an ultimate ground is itself the error, and the work is to cure you of the idea. Those are not the same warning, and the whole of our disagreement lives in the gap between them.

**MAREK.** Perhaps the tension runs through the traditions more deeply than either of us can settle tonight. There are moments when emptiness seems to cleanse the very thing you are trying to protect, and moments when it seems to take it away.

**TIAGO.** That is exactly the hinge.

**MAREK.** Then put it this way. If we agree on the practice, and we agree love is real and binding, and we agree that what a life comes to is what you do with it rather than what you say about it, then perhaps your wall is a choice of pictures. Walls, fixed points, lines cut in stone, against threads, currents, an unfolding. Both pictures can hold the same life upright. And the wall picture can travel in company with certainties that make the grasping mind more dangerous, while the thread picture leans toward the humbler, less grasping way. If we are only choosing pictures, why not the one with fewer ways to go wrong?

**TIAGO.** Because it is more than a choice of pictures, and here is how I know. When I stopped holding love as a line I had chosen and started treating it as something actually there, my judgment about everything downstream got sharper, not looser. A useful fiction can organize a life, even console one. This did something else. It made reality more demanding, and some evasions impossible. I did not draw the line. I walked into something, and the whole room came into focus behind it.

**MAREK.** Or you simply committed to something noble, and any wholehearted commitment to a noble thing can sharpen a life.

**TIAGO.** Then again I ask what makes it noble, not merely intense or useful or consoling. If love widens the world while other commitments narrow it, then the difference cannot be only that I happen to have chosen this one with more force. The widening is part of the evidence. It tells me that this commitment is answerable to something beyond my choosing. Even if it holds across every recognizably human standpoint, a supreme human orientation would not be enough. It would explain why love organizes us, but not why love judges us. It would explain why we gather around it, but not why our evasions of it appear as evasions. The very distinction between true and false orientation already presupposes that some orientations answer more faithfully to reality than others. Love is the one in which the reality of the other becomes least deniable.

**MAREK.** Beyond your choosing, yes. Beyond all relation, no.

**TIAGO.** I am not asking for beyond all relation. That may be the misunderstanding that has haunted us from the start. I am asking whether there is something within relation that is more than relation’s temporary arrangement. A line you can draw and undraw is not the same object as a wall you cannot move. Your own refusal to let love be taken apart is already doing the heavy work. If you then wrap that refusal in empty, groundless, unfolding, you have quietly changed what love is, even if you change nothing you will do tomorrow morning. Whether the wall is there is not a flourish. It is the difference between a life aimed at something real and a life whose aim has no final judge.

**MAREK.** And I hear in that sentence exactly the danger I have been trying to name. You want the judge so badly that you may turn love itself into an idol.

**TIAGO.** Yes. That is the danger on my side. I see it. But there is a danger on yours too. You may become so disciplined against idolatry that you empty the altar even of what deserves to be there.

**MAREK.** So where does that leave the two of us?

**TIAGO.** Sharing the discipline, not the ontology. You are right that clutching is dangerous and needs a lifelong discipline set against it, and that traditions which lose that discipline grow the very pathologies I would never defend. I am right, or at least I cannot stop thinking I am right, that something has to be binding in a way we do not merely authorize, or the whole thing collapses into constructions we can renegotiate when they become inconvenient. And a life built only on renegotiable construction is not the life either of us actually lives, whatever we say after the second glass.

**MAREK.** Which would mean neither picture is safe on its own.

TIAGO. Perhaps not. Reality may be stranger than either picture can hold alone. Solid enough to bang your head against, and fluid enough to be falsified the moment you call it a thing. Any single image does too much or too little. Perhaps the traditions that survive are the ones that keep enough images alive to correct each other down the centuries. &nbsp;

**MAREK.** And that is still our disagreement. For me, the path can test the commitment without turning it into a ground. You keep translating authority into ground, as if a test only has force when something stands behind it like a pillar. I think authority can be lived, enacted, corrected, and deepened without being secured in that way.

**TIAGO.** But if the test answers to nothing real, I do not see why it has authority. If compassion, truthfulness, and self emptying are the tests, then I want to know why those tests are more than preferences internal to a path.

**MAREK.** Perhaps because a relation is not arbitrary just because it is relational.

**TIAGO.** No. But neither is it authoritative simply because it changes us.

**MAREK.** Then perhaps that is the point we cannot get past.

**TIAGO.** Yes. You trust the testing of the path without asking it to rest on ground.

**MAREK.** And you think the test itself is unintelligible unless something real answers through it.

**TIAGO.** I do.

**MAREK.** Then we have found the disagreement clearly enough.

**TIAGO.** Clearly, at least.

**MAREK.** Perhaps that is all we get tonight.
