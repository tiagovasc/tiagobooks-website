John Vervaeke is a cognitive scientist, philosopher, and creator of the acclaimed series Awakening from the Meaning Crisis. In this conversation, he traces the origin of the Anagoge Podcast's name back to Plato's concept of spiritual ascent, then unpacks what genuine dialogos is and why it is so rarely achieved. He explains how a distributed flow state between people can access collective intelligence and afford self-transcendence, and why the same capacity is lost in online formats. Vervaeke recounts his own intellectual journey from fundamentalist Christianity through militant atheism to non-theism, and how encounters with Socrates, Buddhist practice, and cognitive science converged into the work on meaning and wisdom. He offers a compact strategy for bridging the religious and atheist worldviews through the lens of self-deception rather than doctrine. He defends a nuanced position on mystical experience: that its propositional content deserves serious attention while its epistemic weight is constrained by the state that generates it. He addresses the dangers of self-selection in spiritual practice and how both bottom-up design features and the collective rationality of dialogos serve as checks. And he closes with a rigorous account of how to build deep interdisciplinary knowledge, reading in quartets, interacting with texts, and choosing a path through cognitive science that does not require heavy mathematics.

## Who Is John Vervaeke?

John Vervaeke is Professor of Psychology, Cognitive Science, and Buddhist Psychology at the University of Toronto. He is the creator and host of Awakening from the Meaning Crisis, a fifty-episode lecture series that has reached millions of viewers worldwide. His academic work focuses on relevance realization, intelligence, rationality, wisdom, and the cognitive science of mindfulness and transformative experience. He holds a PhD in cognitive psychology and has practiced Tai Chi, Chi Kung, and Vipassana meditation for over thirty years. He is a central figure in the dialogos and participatory knowing revival alongside Christopher Mastropietro, Philip Verviski, and others.

## Top 3 Theses

•&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Dialogos is not simply a good conversation but a distributed flow state in which people access collective intelligence, resonate into deeper layers of self-knowledge, and can afford genuine self-transcendence, an experience that consistently elicits reverence regardless of the participants' religious or secular backgrounds.

•&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; The most productive way to bridge the religious-atheist divide is not to engage the doctrinal or knowledge arena at all, but to shift both parties into the wisdom arena by asking what practices they use to overcome self-deception, a challenge neither group can easily deflect.

•&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; The propositions that emerge from mystical experience deserve serious consideration as candidates for truth about phenomenological and relational reality, but the state that generates the experience heavily predicts which propositions emerge, which constrains their independent epistemic weight.

## Key Insights

**1. Dialogos creates a distributed flow state that transcends individual cognition.** When mindful self-disclosure, mindsight into the other, and authentic mutual attunement combine, a dynamical system emerges that takes on a life of its own. Participants arrive at insights they could not have reached individually, and the experience consistently produces reverence, what Vervaeke and Christopher Mastropietro call the "we space" or diced logos. This is not metaphor; it is a real property of distributed cognition at its most integrated.

**2. Online formats structurally inhibit genuine dialogos.** Two constraints make deep dialogos difficult in podcast formats: the absence of embodied presence, and the need to keep a third audience in mind. Vervaeke's remedy is to treat the audience as a Greek chorus rather than an obstacle, observers whose presence can enrich rather than restrict the conversation, and to build a meta-dialogue across many episodes so that different viewers find their own access points.

**3. The path from fundamentalism through militant atheism to non-theism is a recognizable cultural journey.** Vervaeke's own biography, traumatizing religious upbringing, radicalized departure, encounter with Socrates as an alternative positive frame, Buddhist practice, and finally cognitive science, mirrors what demographic research confirms is increasingly common. Platonic philosophy was ideally suited to someone needing the connections that religion promises without its doctrinal constraints, because it was itself originally replacing the religion of ancient Greece.

**4. The wisdom lens, not the knowledge lens, is where the religious and atheist worldviews can meet.** Asking an atheist what they actually practice to overcome self-deception, not what they believe, creates a productive existential challenge because science is itself about overcoming self-deception and they know it. Asking a fundamentalist to describe their experience of sin, rather than its doctrinal definition, reveals surprising overlap with secular accounts of self-deception. Both moves shift people away from the locked narrative of eternal opposition.

**5. Mystical experience has propositional import, but the state predicts the proposition.** Vervaeke acknowledges he overstated the case for ignoring propositional content from mystical experience. The more careful view is that propositions about phenomenological connectedness and existential meaning deserve weight as plausible candidates for truth. However, the consistent finding that psychedelic-induced experiences generate impersonal descriptions of ultimate reality while non-psychedelic mystical experiences generate theistic ones shows that the generating state, not independent contact with reality, heavily predicts the resulting propositions.

**6. An ecology of spiritual practices requires both bottom-up design constraints and top-down collective wisdom.** The risk of self-selection in spiritual autodidacticism, gravitating only toward what confirms existing preferences, mirrors Augustine's warning that following only the parts of the Gospel you like means following yourself, not the Gospel. Vervaeke's solution: choose practices with evidence for their strengths and weaknesses, build in complementary checks and balances (mindfulness and active open-mindedness should always be practiced together), and let the dialogos community function as the collective normative guide that churches once provided.

**7. Reading in quartets is the most powerful method for building deep interdisciplinary knowledge.** Rather than reading one book at a time, Vervaeke reads four thematically related books in parallel. Initially harder, the method produces a positive manifold: the books begin to dialogue with each other, generating questions and corrections across texts, and drawing the reader ever deeper into each one. This is how he has built knowledge across philosophy, cognitive science, religion, psychology, and the history of ideas over decades, combined with marginal annotation and treating each text as a conversation partner rather than a passive source.
