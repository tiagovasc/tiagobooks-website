**_A book review of&nbsp;‘_A Passion for Nature: The Life of John Muir_’ by_ Donald Worster**

I hadn’t known who John Muir was, never having heard the name before, but I kept seeing people mention this as an excellent biography and the nature theme appealed to me. For those who were in my shoes, John Muir was a very influential naturalist at the end of the 19th century and is often considered the father of the modern conservation movement. The biography starts with his father, a strict disciplinarian who was Scottish, before moving with the family to the United States when Muir was still a child. This context provided insight into his family life and the severe Calvinist upbringing that influenced his views, as he often discussed in his writings, even though I found the sheer volume of information on his early years somewhat excessive. The bulk of the story really begins with Muir in his early twenties, when he first joined the university and started to become interested in botany. Although he didn’t finish his degree, this marked the true start of his life as a nature lover, despite the roots of his passion being much older. Muir was fond of hiking—famously walking a thousand miles to the Gulf—and the descriptions of nature that he wrote inspired the American people and eventually brought him fame.

The narrative flows well, making it easy to place oneself in Muir’s shoes as he evolves from a wandering botanist into a political icon. The book is far from a simple collection of facts or events. Rather, it successfully illustrates his comprehensive approach to nature, and it is hard not to be captivated by the way Worster weaves Muir’s private journals with his public persona. I found his personality compelling because I could easily identify with his worldview and the decisions he made. He certainly had flaws, as we all do, and was a product of his time in terms of social justice, yet it is hard to dislike him. He genuinely cared about nature, and I was especially captivated by how his awe of the wild was both scientific and spiritual. For him, they were deeply intertwined rather than opposing forces. Despite nature being his primary pursuit for most of the time, and his tendency to spend significant parts of his youth traveling alone, he nevertheless had a rich social and family life, particularly as he aged. Even though he was naturally averse to politics, he cultivated a political life when trying to support the conservation movement. In fact, many attribute the success of modern conservation to Muir, and the first national parks, such as Yosemite, were established under the heavy influence of his writings and activism.

What I didn’t expect, and partly what I enjoyed the most about the biography, was the deep immersion into the time he lived. Many of his life’s struggles are hard to fully comprehend in a modern context because we now take so much of what he fought for granted, so I appreciated that I was receiving a history lesson about the end of the 19th century in America while reading. It was fascinating to witness the societal problems they were grappling with, specifically the tension between rapid industrialization and the preservation of the landscape. Although the context is different today, the primary struggle relevant to Muir is still profoundly relevant: our perception of nature, balancing the pros and cons of capitalism, and the relationship between nature and spirituality. It also aptly demonstrates the psychology of the Reformation and its impact on the culture of the time, as well as its influence on perceptions of capitalism and nature. It was an engaging book and I am glad to have read it. It is rather long, which should be taken into account, but I believe the investment pays off. While John Muir’s name may not be very well-known outside the U.S. or among naturalist enthusiasts, his legacy continues to live on in our culture. This book beautifully presents that legacy with the added bonus of a richer understanding of the time he lived in and the eternal relationship between human beings and nature.
