John Vervaeke, Julian Kiverstein and Hüseyin Beyköylü join me to discuss how relevance realization and enactive sense-making connect cognition with meaning, wisdom and transformation. We examine metastability as an account of how embodied engagement can sustain skills while allowing changes in how we relate to the world.

We debate whether predictive processing and the free energy principle can explain these capacities, and what distinguishes mathematical modeling from explaining cognition. These questions extend to LLMs: their dependence on human relevance realization, the limits of linguistic performance, and their potential to support dialogue and collective intelligence without weakening human judgment.

## Who are the guests?

**John Vervaeke** develops the account of relevance realization discussed in the episode. His work considers how we find what matters in a situation and how that capacity bears on wisdom and the meaning crisis.

**Julian Kiverstein** works at the intersection of philosophy and cognitive science. He draws on ecological psychology and enactive approaches to explain skilled action and explores how predictive processing might fit with those approaches.

**Hüseyin Beyköylü** brings enactive philosophy and dynamical systems to the study of cognition and transformative experience. The conversation begins with his account of the cognitive continuum, which he developed with Vervaeke and Daniel Meling.

## Top Takeaways

1. Skill, insight, flow and transformative experience can be understood through changes in how a person engages with their surroundings, at different scales.
2. Metastability gives the guests a shared problem to explain. They disagree about whether predictive processing and enaction can explain it on compatible terms.
3. Language models can help people explore and develop ideas. The guests debate what sort of understanding that interaction involves and who supplies the judgment.

## Key Insights

**What matters depends on the situation.** A living being needs to respond to some possibilities among the many its surroundings offer. Relevance realization names that ongoing selection. Kiverstein connects it to skilled action, where an experienced person can sense when their grip on a situation needs adjusting.

**Transformative experiences can reorganize a life.** The cognitive continuum groups experiences by the scale of the change they involve. A moment of fluency can improve how someone handles a task. A transformation can change which possibilities they see and what matters to them across many situations.

**Stability can make change possible.** A metastable pattern holds for a time and remains able to shift. The guests discuss this at the level of a person acting in an environment. Both can change, and their interaction helps determine which patterns persist and which become possible next.

**The dispute concerns what models explain.** Kiverstein asks whether the free energy principle can describe a system that stays viable through changes in its relation to the environment. Beyköylü presses on the assumptions behind that proposal, and Vervaeke questions when a formal description warrants a claim about the underlying mechanism.

**AI can support inquiry while depending on human judgment.** The group considers how a language model can help someone articulate a thought, draw connections or find a new line of inquiry. They also ask how the human participant evaluates what the model offers and what ecological costs come with using it.
