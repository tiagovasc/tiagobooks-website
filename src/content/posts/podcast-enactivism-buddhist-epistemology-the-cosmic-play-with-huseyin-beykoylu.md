Hüseyin Beyköylü left medicine for cognitive science after an LSD experience, and five years later he's arguing that mainstream psychology and neuroscience are built on assumptions that systematically miss what matters most about the mind. This is a dense, careful conversation about four ways of knowing (only one of which science actually measures), why randomized controlled trials can't capture human transformation, and why "psychedelics are not transformative, lived experience in a context is."&nbsp;

We get into the entropy-fluency hypothesis, how destabilization and reorganization cycle through every scale from insight to mystical experience, and why false fluency (the smoothness of a conspiracy theory, the comfort of a simplified worldview) is just as real as the real thing. In the second half, the conversation opens into metaphysics: what is truth, is there a ground, and does groundlessness mean nothing matters? Hüseyin draws on Madhyamaka, Spinoza, Karen Barad, and Varela to argue that reality is a cosmic play we actively participate in, and that truth is something you embody rather than correspond to. The episode closes with a thought experiment about what wisdom one would offer one's younger self, and a reflection on why suffering is often the crawl through the shit that makes clean possible. &nbsp;

## **Who’s** Hüseyin Beyköylü **?**

Hüseyin Beyköylü is a cognitive scientist from Cyprus and a 5th year PhD candidate at Leiden University's PRiSM lab. Holding a 1st class medical degree and an MSc in Brain Sciences, his research explores the mind through medicine, neuroscience, and philosophy. Grounded in 4E cognitive science and complex systems theory, he investigates psychedelic, mystical, and self-transcendent experiences.&nbsp;

## Top 3 theses

**The limits of propositional knowing:** Western cognitive science has fallen into propositional tyranny, overprivileging a narrow, fact-stating mode of knowing that depends on three deeper, largely ineffable modes (procedural, perspectival, participatory); any adequate science of the mind must take these non-propositional kinds of knowing as constitutive rather than auxiliary. &nbsp;

**Transformation is context-dependent, not substance-driven:** The more you attribute transformation to the intervention, the more you miss why the same drug, the same retreat, the same practice produces insight in one person and psychiatric breakdown in another. &nbsp;

**Groundlessness does not entail nihilism:** Madhyamaka's groundlessness, reality's emptiness of intrinsic existence, does not entail anti-realism, relativism, or nihilism; it entails that we are active participants in a process-relational reality, where knowing, doing, and being are mutually constituting, and where truth is embodied faithfulness rather than correspondence to a thing.

## Memorable Quotes

> "Reality is groundless, the self is groundless, the world is groundless. Reality is empty, but not nothing. It's empty of intrinsic existence, meaning there are no things. The self is not a thing, the world is not a thing. There is no ultimate, intrinsically existent, independent thing"&nbsp;

> "When you take a participatory epistemology, it goes hand-in-hand with a relational ontology and with ethical know-how. Karen Barad calls this ethico-onto-epistemology. Knowing, doing, and being can't be separated. Participatory knowing is knowing by being. The knower and the known were never separate; that separation was always empty."&nbsp;

> "Truth is deeper than the propositional. You can live truly. You do not merely utter the teachings or embrace them propositionally; you give your life to them. When you participate in that faithfulness, you embody truth, because you are becoming it, realizing it, in communion with it"

## **Key Insights**

1. **&nbsp;Participatory knowing is the foundation, not the ceiling.** In the revised 4P model, participatory knowing is ontologically foundational; propositional, procedural, and perspectival knowing are evolutionary exaptations of the more basic fact that a living system co-enacts itself and its world through its autonomy.

2. **The ergodicity assumption breaks for complex human phenomena.** Statistical methods in psychology implicitly assume ergodicity, that group averages and individual time courses carry comparable information, but this assumption fails precisely where clinical stakes are highest: psychiatric, existential, and meaning-laden phenomena are non-stationary, history-dependent, and deeply context-sensitive.

3. **Fluency can be maladaptive.** The felt sense of "things fitting," fluency, is not intrinsically good; it is normatively charged in a context-dependent way. Oversimplified models of reality, conspiracy theories, and spiritual narcissism all produce strong fluency while untethering the person from lived reality.

4. **Neurophenomenology is a remedy, not a solution.** Varela proposed neurophenomenology not as a reductive account of consciousness but as a Wittgensteinian "remedy" that dissolves the hard problem's Cartesian premises. The practice is mutual constraint between rich first-person description and rich third-person data, not explanation of one by the other.

5. **Truth is embodied faithfulness, not just correspondence.** A participatory, process-relational framework reframes truth as a lived category: one "is" true by being faithful to a way of life, embodying values, and enacting alignment with what one claims to know, rather than by merely uttering correct propositions.
