Danny Forde is a philosopher and lecturer at University College Cork whose 2024 book _Phenomenology of Psychedelic Experiences_ argues that psychedelics give direct contact with mind-independent features of reality. He grounds the claim in realist phenomenology, the Munich-Göttingen tradition that rejected Husserl's idealist turn after 1905, and defends what Joseph Seifert called sober Platonism: essences are real, but not floating in a separate realm of forms. The conversation runs through anti-reductionism via Husserl's anti-psychologism, the failure of representationalism, the tripartite structure of the self with for-me-ness as the irreducible minimal core, and the claim that psychedelics reveal essences once the ego stops cognitively penetrating perception. Tiago pushes back at length on essentialism, perennialism, and the place of existential orientation against perception, with Danny defending essentialism and a Sawyer-style perennialism that takes traditions seriously without collapsing them.&nbsp;

## **Who’s** Danny Forde?

Danny Forde is an assistant lecturer at University College Cork, teaching in the religion and philosophy departments. He completed his PhD in 2023 on the phenomenology of psychedelic experiences and published the book of the same title in 2024. He is training as a psychotherapist and works in the realist phenomenology tradition. &nbsp;

## Top 3 theses

- **Psychedelics give direct contact with mind-independent essences.** When the ego is suspended, what's left is what Danny calls "ego-free seeing": perception without the pragmatic and narrative impositions that normally cognitively penetrate experience, putting you in direct touch with the flowerness of flowers, the essence of love, and the ground of being.

- **The minimal self persists through ego dissolution.** The brain-as-fiction account of selfhood from Letheby and Millière mistakes the narrative ego for the whole self. For-me-ness, the bare pre-reflective givenness of experience, is structurally necessary to having any experience at all, so it cannot dissolve.

- **Naturalism without consciousness is not real naturalism.** Scientific naturalism that excludes subjective experience leaves out the one thing science cannot do without. Real naturalism (after Galen Strawson) is enriched: it takes consciousness, values, and essences as part of the cosmos to be intuited, not reduced away.

## Memorable Quotes

> "If we all die and a slime mold on Jupiter evolves into something that can do math, in a few years they'll figure out the Pythagorean theorem because those things are eternally valid and true. They won't say the words 'Pythagorean theorem' because Pythagoras is dead, but the thing they're intuiting will still exist."

> "What psychedelics do is reorder your ordo amoris, your order of love. Before a psychedelic experience, your order of love can be shaped by your familial background, your culture, your society. A psychedelic experience reorders that and puts it more in tune with what we might call alignment with the logos."

> "If you deny essences, you have to get rid of love, beauty, and art. Can you explain a Beethoven symphony in purely physical terms? Vibrations, then a gap, then more vibrations. But it produces a visceral emotional reaction. A painting on your wall is just patches of color, if you even think color exists."

## Key Insights

**Real naturalism takes consciousness seriously.** Scientific naturalism claims that physics, once finished, will explain everything including subjective experience. Danny argues that you cannot have an explanation of experience that excludes experience itself. After Galen Strawson, real naturalism includes consciousness, values, and essences in what counts as real.

**Realist phenomenology is sober Platonism.** Joseph Seifert's phrase. The Munich-Göttingen Circle around Scheler, Stein, and Ingarden held that essences are real but not floating in a separate realm of forms. The flowerness of flowers is in the flowers, intuitable in perception, not hidden behind a metaphysical curtain.

**Ego-free seeing is the heart of psychedelic insight.** Ordinary perception is cognitively penetrated by the narrative ego: history, plans, identity, pragmatic concerns. Psychedelics temporarily send the ego on holiday, removing those impositions. What remains is direct contact with things as they are, which Danny calls ego-free seeing.

**For-me-ness cannot dissolve, because it is constitutive of experience.** The neuroskeptic claim that the self is a Cartesian fiction conflates the narrative ego (which does dissolve) with the minimal self (the bare pre-reflective for-me-ness of experience). For-me-ness is not added to experience; it is structurally part of any experience whatsoever.

**The ground of being is here, not elsewhere.** Following the Gospel of Thomas, "The kingdom of God is spread upon the earth and men do not see it." Psychedelics do not transport you to a separate metaphysical realm. They drop the ego enough to reveal what is already present in this conversation, in a vase of flowers, in a dog whining at the door.
